    Limitations¶

    These are measured or recorded limits, in the repository's own words where it has them. Read them before treating a result as a statement about the real world.

    Simulation and model¶

    • No hardware. Nothing here has been checked against a field node. A same-site hardware test (TECH_DEBT T45) is open.
    • Receiver sensitivity is unverified end to end. A six-decibel indexing error (sf - 7 against tables whose index 0 is SF5) sat on the tier carrying about 95 % of active cycles and passed every gate, verifier check and validation in the project. An independent closed-form model refusing to reconcile found it. No check in the project would catch a new error of that kind. Sensitivities are now read from data-sheet rows, but that is a reading, not a check.
    • Unattributed residuals. About 3.7 dB on UWB and 3.5 dB for 868 MHz near field remain unexplained; conclusions are drawn by recomputing under ±4 dB of margin offset chosen to bracket them.
    • Loss terms computed and discarded. Four loss terms (antenna gain, Doppler penalty, multipath penalty, soil attenuation) are assigned 0.0 on the line after they are computed, in the caller; in one configuration eight of sixteen terms in the loss sum were structurally zero.
    • Effective sensitivity is not a constant. A 1.5 dB link-hysteresis margin applies whenever the link was previously receiving, outside the loss sum, so the reported margin's denominator moves with the outcome and can mimic a distance-dependent effect.
    • Terrain is procedural and was not rescoped (gate G11 stays red by decision). Foliage depth is clamped to the link length.
    • Propagation assumptions. The path-loss exponent and shadowing values are engineering assumptions with no traced field source.
    • The NLOS detector is circular by construction. On a simulated channel it can only rediscover the obstructions the channel model created; its measured precision on one audit was 0.02–0.08.
    • The tier-1 ranging error is large: 1543 m mean at the distance where delivery falls to 0.8. Tier 1 is a coverage floor, and applying a time-of-arrival Cramér–Rao bound to it would be a category error (an open defect: the CRLB gate should not run on tier 1).
    • One random sampler ignores its seed (lora-distribution-sampler.cc); determinism holds and no result is known to be affected.
    • The evidence gate's 20 m radius lies beyond UWB's reach (delivery 0.14 at 20 m).

    The decision layer¶

    • The escalation rule is not shown to beat a flat variance threshold; the score carries no information beyond the covariance trace.
    • The predicted-benefit rule \(\Delta J/C>\lambda\) is specified and not evaluated.
    • The integrity check does not detect most injected faults and was removed from the preset.
    • The thresholds and the dwell are fitted or the project's own choice; none was swept across environments.

    The evaluation¶

    • The 5 km margin is thin: 0.952 (CI [0.911, 0.975]; 18 of 20 runs).
    • Results hold for one environment (forested, dense vegetation) and one geometry family. Different terrain, density or subject behaviour has not been evaluated.
    • The requirement is on position error within 25 m; time to first fix and convergence are not measured.
    • The deployment that meets the requirement (12 gateways, 12 finders) is not the brief's; it was found by measuring gateways × finders.
    • Energy is radio energy only; processing energy is not modelled.
    • A self-deploying gateway mesh is not implemented and is outside the brief's fixed deployment.

    Regulatory¶

    The simulator models the Indian 865–867 MHz duty-cycle rule. Other jurisdictions, and the 2.4 GHz and UWB bands, are not modelled (regulatory considerations).

    Open work with entry points is in docs/WORK_PACKAGES.md and docs/TECH_DEBT_STATUS.md.
