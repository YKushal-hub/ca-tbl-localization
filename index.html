    CA-TBL¶

    Covariance-Aware Tier-Based Logic for GPS-denied search and rescue. An ns-3.47 simulation of three radio tiers, 868 MHz LoRa, 2.4 GHz LoRa time of flight and UWB, with a decision layer that escalates between them and a per-action energy model traceable to datasheet currents.

    Install and build Architecture Results Source

    What this is, and what it is not

    This is a simulation and its measurement apparatus. Every number on this site comes from a registered campaign in the repository, run under --build-profile=optimized. No field test of the radio models has been made against hardware. The system's name is expanded in the repository's GitHub description as Covariance-Aware; older documents in the repository, and the code's identifiers (pcs*), still say confidence. They name the same system and the same score; see the uncertainty score.

    The problem¶

    Search and rescue in GPS-denied terrain needs both long reach and fine ranging, and no single radio delivers both. Range wants low frequency and narrow bandwidth; precision wants wide bandwidth, which shortens reach and, on a DW3000-class UWB part, makes the receive current (94 mA) larger than the transmit current (82 mA), so a precision exchange is spent mostly listening. A wearable's battery rules out keeping that radio on. The design answer is a hierarchy of radios plus a decision layer that spends precision only where it is useful and reachable. More.

    Key concepts¶

    tier radio role measured reach, r80
    1 868 MHz LoRa, SX1262 wide-area coverage floor; RSSI ranges, TDoA where three gateways hear a broadcast 2390 m
    2 2.4 GHz LoRa, SX1280 mid-range time-of-flight refinement 320 m
    3 UWB, DW3000 short-range precision, double-sided two-way ranging 5.4 m

    r80 is the distance at which 80 % of frames are received, measured by the simulator's range probe under the evaluated configuration (Radios).

    Every node carries all three radios. A tier is a choice of radio, not of peer. Nodes are gateways (fixed, surveyed), finders (mobile searchers with GNSS) and subjects (the field nodes being located). A five-state inertial filter carries each subject's position between fixes. The decision layer escalates one tier when a covariance-based uncertainty score falls below a per-tier threshold, subject to two suppress-only checks. Escalation.

    The design requirement, and where it stands¶

    A ≥ 0.95 at R ≤ 25 m: the share of all decision cycles with a position within 25 m. A cycle with no fix counts against it.

    configuration (CA-TBL, 12 gateways, 12 finders) tag 0 five-node pool
    5 km 0.986 0.952
    endgame 0.999 0.993

    Source: PRESET-5, registered, 20 fresh seeds. With the brief's three gateways the five-node pool reaches 0.875 at 5 km, and no finder count up to 24 closes the gap. The 5 km margin is modest. The tiered system is level with flat-threshold switching and with a static 2.4 GHz arm on availability; what it buys is against a static 868 MHz arm and, against a POUCET-style fusion of all radios, the same availability at about 1/34 of the subject energy at 5 km. All results, with intervals.

    Features confirmed in the repository¶

    • Three physics engines (868 MHz, 2.4 GHz, UWB) with about 35 submodules each, and emergent collisions with a 6 dB capture rule.
    • A five-state IMU filter (position, heading offset, gyro bias, stride scale) with a 60-cycle window smoother and particle-filter acquisition from an unknown start.
    • Relay-aware uncertainty inflation, σ²eff = σ² + uᵀPBu.
    • A per-action energy model: charged per radio action from datasheet currents, with one tier charged per cycle.
    • Registered campaigns with a provenance sidecar (run_id.txt, SHA-256 per output) and 166 regression gates.
